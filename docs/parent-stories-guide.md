# Parent stories – interview guide and consent forms

*Version 1 · October 2026 · Part of `docs/marketing-strategy.md` (section 4.3)*
*Three real stories that prove what Julie's story promises. They replace the sample testimonials on the website.*

---

## 1. The three stories

| Story | Who | What it proves | Used on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. The fresh start** | Parent of a child who arrived with little or no English | "Here, a child is never held back for a language gap." | Home, Parents page, ads |
| **B. The safe harbor** | Parent of a child who needed individual support (learning difference, anxiety, a hard time at a previous school) | "Your child is known and supported." | Parents page, Special education page |
| **C. The bridge to the world** | An AGI graduate (adult) now at university or working abroad – and, if possible, their parent | "An AGI education opens doors." | Home, Pathways, About |

Aim for families from different backgrounds (Moroccan and international) across the three stories.

---

## 2. Finding the families

- Julie and the teachers suggest 5–6 families; choose 3. Prefer families who **already speak warmly about AGI** – never pressure anyone.
- First contact by Julie or the class teacher, in the family's preferred language (English, French, Arabic or Darija).
- Explain clearly: what the story is for, where it will appear, that it's voluntary and unpaid, and that they approve the final version and can withdraw.
- For special-needs stories (B): extra care. The family decides what is shared; no diagnosis is named unless they want it; the child's face can stay off camera.

**Pitch to the family (short):**
> "We're telling the story of how AGI began – one room, five children in 2013 – and we'd love parents to tell, in their own words, what the school has meant for their child. It takes about 30 minutes, you'll see everything before it's published, and you can say no or change your mind at any time."

---

## 3. Before the interview

- [ ] Signed consent forms (section 7) – parent, and child's assent if the child appears.
- [ ] 10-minute call: what happened, in their words. Don't script them.
- [ ] Agree the format: **video** (2–3 minutes raw → 45–60 s edit) or **written** (150–200 words + one photo).
- [ ] Agree how they are named: first name, "Parent of a grade 3 student", or anonymous.
- [ ] Choose a calm place: their home, the school, or a quiet café. Natural light.
- [ ] Interviewer: someone the family knows (a teacher) or a friendly outsider – **not Julie**, so parents speak freely.

## 4. Interview questions

**Rules for the interviewer:** open questions only · let silences happen · ask "Can you give me an example?" · never suggest words · ask them to repeat the question in their answer ("When we first came to AGI…").

### Opening (all stories)
1. Tell me a little about your family and your child.
2. How did you first hear about AGI?
3. What were you looking for in a school at that time?

### A. The fresh start (language)
4. How much English did your child speak when they started?
5. How did you feel before the first day? What were you worried about?
6. What happened in the first weeks?
7. Was there a moment when you noticed a change? What was it?
8. How is your child's English now – at school, at home, with friends?
9. What would you say to a parent whose child doesn't speak English yet?

### B. The safe harbor (support)
4. What was school like for your child before AGI?
5. What did you need from a school that you weren't finding?
6. How did AGI respond when you explained your child's needs?
7. How do the teachers and the school work with you as a family?
8. How is your child different today – at school and at home?
9. What would you tell a parent who is worried that their child won't fit in?

### C. The bridge to the world (graduate)
4. How old were you when you came to AGI, and what was it like?
5. What do you remember most – a teacher, a moment, a habit?
6. How did AGI prepare you for what you're doing now?
7. Where are you now, and what are you studying or doing?
8. What did your English, your diploma or your Anglia certificate change for you?
9. What would you say to a family deciding whether to choose AGI?

### Closing (all stories)
10. If you had to describe AGI in three words, what would they be?
11. Is there anything we didn't ask that you'd like to say?

### For children (optional, ages 7+, with a parent present, 5 minutes)
- What do you like most about your school?
- Tell me about your favorite teacher.
- What's something you learned this year that you're proud of?
- How do you feel when you walk into school in the morning?

Never ask a child about difficulties, diagnoses or a previous school.

## 5. Turning interviews into stories

**Video (45–60 seconds)**
1. Opening: who they are, in one sentence.
2. Before: the worry or the difficulty.
3. The turning point: what AGI did, with a concrete example.
4. Now: the change, in their words.
5. Advice to another parent.
6. End card: *Affordable · Creative · Inclusive* · **Book a visit** · WhatsApp +212 688 739 393.

**Written (150–200 words + photo)**
- Headline from their own words (e.g. *"Two years later, she corrects my pronunciation."*).
- 3 short paragraphs: before → turning point → now.
- One pull quote for the website testimonial card.

**Editing rules**
- Use only their words. Cut, don't rewrite. Light grammar fixes in written versions only.
- No promises the school can't keep, and nothing that contradicts verified facts.
- The family approves the final version **in writing** before publication.
- Subtitles in English, French and Arabic (as for the founder film).

**On the website** – replace the sample testimonials in `v2/_build/pages.py` (`TESTIMONIALS`) with the approved quotes; the pre-launch check stops flagging them once the `data-placeholder="testimonial:…"` markers are gone.
