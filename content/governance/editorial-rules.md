# AGI editorial and publication rules

## Source hierarchy
1. Current signed agreements, regulator-issued authorisations, approved school project, official awarding-body specifications and current approved policies.
2. Current official provider/partner pages.
3. AGI public pages and archived material, treated as evidence of what was published rather than proof that a claim remains current.
4. Third-party directories and search snippets, used only for discovery.

## Claim handling
Every material claim must have an owner, source, review date and status. Use verified-source only when a reliable source supports the wording; this does not replace AGI approval. Use confirmation-required when internal evidence is needed. Use hold when publication could mislead or create regulatory, consumer or safeguarding risk.

## Distinguish organisations and qualifications
- Distinguish AGI, Anglia Examinations, AIM, Ofqual, Chichester College Group, AWA, Bridge and all other providers.
- State who teaches, who assesses, who awards the qualification and who regulates/accredits it.
- Describe AGI's role only as supported by a current agreement or approved operating procedure.
- Do not imply that a partner's accreditation automatically applies to every AGI activity.

## High-risk content
Fees, dates, admissions criteria, grade scope, school authorisation, foreign-education permissions, qualification recognition, health/therapy services, safeguarding, proctoring, refund/cancellation terms and third-party pathways require current documentary verification.

## Testimonials and media
Never fabricate testimonials, case studies, student numbers or outcomes. Obtain appropriate written consent for identifiable people, especially minors. Record image rights, permitted channels, expiry/withdrawal terms and accurate alt text.

## Localisation
English is the master. French and Arabic are adaptations, not literal translations. Keep official qualification and organisation names unchanged. Arabic pages must declare RTL direction. Review local legal terminology against authoritative Moroccan Arabic sources.
